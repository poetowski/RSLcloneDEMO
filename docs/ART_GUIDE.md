# Art Guiderails

This is the rulebook for every pixel in The Loom: Reliquary of Legends. Champions, effects, combat backgrounds, the world map, icons and UI parts are all **generated by code** (`tools/art/*`, run `npm run art`), and the generator enforces most of these rules automatically. `npm run audit` proves the rest. When you add art, follow the same rules so it sits next to the existing art without looking foreign.

**Direction:** high fantasy with a gothic arcane look. No science fiction, and no literal yarn or craft look: the Loom's threads, spools and patterns are arcane relics, never sewing things. The world is Jakub's ([DESIGN_DECISIONS.md](DESIGN_DECISIONS.md)): a realm broken by the fall of a celestial loom, floating islands, four factions with their standards. The two champions are drawn from his Starter Heroes record (DESIGN_DECISIONS.md 8); Zone 1's background follows his brief (DESIGN_DECISIONS.md 5.3a) and the world map his brief of the same day (5.4a), approved as built ("i love it"); he likes how the game looks as of 2026-10-11; new art is proposed with previews and approved before it is built.

Step-by-step recipes live in the project skills: [new champion](../.claude/skills/new-champion/SKILL.md) and [new combat background](../.claude/skills/new-combat-background/SKILL.md).

## 1. Grid and screen

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Tile | **32 x 32 px** |
| Virtual screen | **640 x 360** (20 x 11.25 tiles) |
| Scaling | whole numbers only (x2 = 1280x720, x3 = 1920x1080); letterbox the rest |
| Sub-pixel positions | never: everything is drawn at integer coordinates in the 640x360 buffer |
| Rotation / scaling of art | only inside the 640x360 buffer, never on the upscaled image (no "mixels"); the only 2x art is the champion showcase (detail page, recruit ceremony), drawn at exactly 2x |
| Smooth gradients, anti-aliased shapes | never; gradients are posterized bands or 4x4 Bayer dithering (`ui.bands`, `ui.glow`, `rampDither`) |

All art is authored at 1x. The game renders into a 640x360 canvas and the browser upscales it with nearest-neighbour filtering.

## 2. Champions

### 2.1 Size and frame box

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Body height (feet to top of head/helmet) | **66-80 px**; the audit fails below **64** or above **96** (idle frame 0) |
| Sprite height incl. weapon/horns/ears at rest | 68-90 px |
| Frame box | **208 x 160 px** for every champion and every frame |
| Pivot | **(104, 140)**: the point between the feet on the ground |
| Facing | champions are generated for **both** facings (R and L); sprites are never mirrored at runtime |
| Ground plane | `death` and `rise` frames are clipped 8 px below the feet line (`GROUND_CLIP`), so capes and sashes rest on the floor |

The frame box is generous so a glaive at full extension or a falling body never clips; the audit fails any frame that touches its edges. The build trims each frame and packs it into an atlas with the offset of the trimmed rect, so empty space costs nothing.

### 2.2 Proportions (about 5 heads tall)

| Part | Size |
| --- | --- |
| Head | 13-15 px tall (animal heads may run longer) |
| Neck to pelvis (torso) | 19.5-22.5 px |
| Legs (hip to ankle) | 31-34 px, slight knee bend at rest |
| Arms | upper 12-13.5, forearm 11-12.5, fist radius ~3 |
| Shoulder width in 3/4 view | 16-20 px including pauldrons |

Heroic stylisation: slightly large head and hands for readability, broad shoulders for warriors, slim silhouettes for casters and rogues.

A hunched champion keeps his height in the hump and the hat (the Jungle Herder: head-top 62.5 px, the hat and the crook to 78 px); a small one in what stands up from it (Big Ears: head-top 41 px, his ears to 64 px, the floor of the audit, since Jakub had them made a little smaller). A beast on four legs is drawn large enough to read: the Bloodhound is waist-high to the others, about 52 px to the withers and 69 px to the top of the head, its body some 55 px long; the Follower, a wolf, about 50 px to the withers and 71 px to the tips of his ears, longer in the leg and in the body; the Black Rhino, a great bull, 66 px to the top of his hump and some 110 px from his tail to the points of his tusks, the roster's largest (`footprint` 2.3). Each casts a longer shadow (`footprint` on its CharDef, 1.9 times a standing champion's) and stands its body a little behind the pivot, so hits and effects land at its chest.

### 2.3 View and layering

- **3/4 side view**, facing the enemy line. The near side of the body faces the camera.
- **Near limbs** are drawn in front of the torso. **Far limbs** are drawn behind it and shaded **one ramp step darker** (`shade: -1`).
- A shield or second weapon on the far arm sits between the torso and the near arm, so the near (weapon) arm always reads on top.
- Capes, manes, wings and quivers sit behind everything; tabards, aprons and kilts sit between the legs.
- Long robes and dresses use `robe()` (`parts.ts`): standing, the cloth hangs straight down with the hem just off the ground; once the wearer tips over (torso under 55 degrees: falling, lying dead) it follows the legs, so it never smears along the floor or leaves the feet behind. Anything else that hangs (sashes, tassels) hangs the same way as the robe.
- Feet stay on the floor: a kneeling back foot rests on its toes, heel up; only a weapon striking or planted may go below the feet line.

Their heads, Gareth's beard, her face and the censer's shape are a first take the record leaves open. `color` in the data is the champion's own hue (Gareth's amber, the Rime Scholar's frost), and so is `iconBg` behind the skill icons and portraits. Within one faction no two champions share a hue family; across factions the material language should separate them. Champions in the field are not walking banners: the standards ([DESIGN_DECISIONS.md](DESIGN_DECISIONS.md) 3.6) belong on banners and sigils. Faction and affinity are independent: the Azure Crown captain is Ember, the Sanguine Dominion ice mage is Tide, on purpose. The Enforcer's crimson and the black hexagon on his chest are Jakub's brief (DESIGN_DECISIONS.md 15): the Sanguine Dominion's standard is a red hexagon on black, and he wears its shape in black on crimson.

**Affinity sets nothing in the art** (Jakub, 2026-10-11: "affinity should not dictate colors or elements or anything. these three affinity choices are very abstract"). Ember is not fire, Bloom is not leaves, Tide is not water: a champion's materials, hue, smears and effects come from who the champion is and what the skill does, and the affinity shows only where the interface marks it (the gem, STRONG HIT and WEAK HIT). The Lineholder is the first champion drawn under the rule: he is Tide, and his mace leaves a plain grey-white streak and lands in a dull flash and dust. Two pieces predated it, Gareth's ember cuts (his own record asked for his Ember "in what he does with the sword", DESIGN_DECISIONS.md 8) and the Skirmisher's leaf-green ones; on 2026-10-12 Jakub had both made plain and kept the interface's affinity marks. Jakub's briefs dress the Skirmisher and the Lineholder both in blue: they part by value (bright blue leather against charcoal and a dark-blue shield) and by silhouette.

## 3. Light

- **One key light for the whole game**: from the **top-left, slightly in front** (`LIGHT_DIR = normalize(-0.5, -0.65, 0.58)` in screen space).
- Every backdrop places its light source in the upper-left to justify it: the dawn sun rising between the far peaks of Zone 1. A second, colored light may live in the sky (Zone 1's rift lights the clouds and the islands' right flanks blue), never on the champions. Tiles are lit the same way (top/left edges bright, bottom/right edges dark), and so are carvings: in sunk relief the upper-left lip of a cut is in shadow and the lower-right wall catches the light.
- Because the light must stay top-left, sprites are **rendered** for both facings instead of mirrored. A mirrored sprite would light its right side.
- Runtime light only adds: additive glow pools around fires (`ZoneDef.glow`), a mood tint (`ZoneDef.tint`), unit shadows offset away from the light (`ZoneDef.shadow`: a low sun throws them longer and further right), and the light of a burning blade (the Paladin's sword, 7.4).

## 4. Palette

Every pixel comes from `tools/art/palette.ts`:

| Table | What | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `MAT` | 6-step material ramps `[outline, deep, shadow, base, light, highlight]` | champions, props drawn with the champion renderer, menu glyphs |
| `ACCENT`, `INK` | single colors for decals, smears, eyes, glyph details | champions, icons |
| `FXR` | 5-step effect ramps, dark edge to white-hot core | combat effects |
| `UIR` | interface ramps: gold, navy, steel, stone, rarity and affinity ramps | UI parts, card frames, gems |

Zones declare their own ramps at the top of their module (sky, stone, sand...), because a background is one painting with its own light; the audit holds them to a color budget instead.

| Index | Use |
| --- | --- |
| outline | 1px silhouette outline and separation lines; never pure black |
| deep | occlusion and far-side core shadow |
| shadow | form shadow |
| base | the local color, what the material "is" |
| light | planes facing the key light |
| highlight | specular sparkle on metal, brightest accents; use sparingly |

**Hue shifting**: shadows move toward blue/violet, highlights toward warm yellow. Never darken a color by just lowering its brightness.

**Champion modules contain no color literals.** `npm run audit` fails a `hex('#...')` or `'#rrggbb'` in `tools/art/champions/*.ts` and any atlas pixel that is not in `MAT`, `ACCENT` or `INK`. Need a new material? Add a 6-step ramp to `MAT` (keep the hue shifting).

![Palette](images/palette.png)

Material kinds decide how light quantizes into the ramp (`BANDS` in `palette.ts`): `matte, cloth, skin, hair, fur, metal, glow`. Metal gets a single-pixel specular highlight; glow materials are unlit and get a colored halo instead of a dark outline.

## 5. Shading

- **Cel bands, no gradients** on characters: the renderer computes a normal per pixel (tubes for limbs, domes for heads, bevels for plates and cloth), takes `n . L`, and snaps it to the material's ramp.
- **No dithering on characters.** Dithering (4x4 Bayer) is for the sky, backdrops, sand and snow, light pools, effects and icon backgrounds.
- **No orphan pixels**: a shade pixel with no same-shade neighbour inside its shape adopts the majority shade (cleanup pass). Deliberate single-pixel highlights are exempt.
- Cloth folds, wraps, bark and scale armour come from per-material texture functions (`tex`), never noise.
- A texture keyed to screen pixels stays put while the shape moves, so on a shape that breathes or sways it swims. Key it to the shape instead: the `Hit` a texture receives carries the shape's own coordinates (`u` along a `cyl` axis, `v` across it; offsets from a `dome`'s centre), as the Black Rhino's coat and mane and the Associate's weave do. Curls read as bunches: small round locks, each lit on its own (the Associate).

## 6. Outlines

- **1px outline around the silhouette**, colored with the outline entry of the material it touches (colored, never black).
- **Separation lines** where one form overlaps another: the pixels of the form behind get the outline color (`strong`) or two ramp steps darker (`soft`, for plate layers and folds).
- Joined parts of the same limb are one group and never get a line between them.
- Separate objects that cross (two swords, a weapon over a shield) each take a group of their own, or they melt together where they overlap; the crossed swords of `mi_campaign` are `sword1` and `sword2`, like the Skirmisher's.
- Glowing parts (crystals, runes, eyes, gems) get a halo in their own deep color instead of a dark outline.

## 7. Animation

### 7.1 Required set per champion

| Animation | Frames | Timing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `idle` | 6-8 | 130-160 ms | whole-pixel breathing (1px), cloth/hair sway; desynchronised per unit |
| `run` | 8 | 65-85 ms | used to approach a target and to return; cape and hair stream back |
| `attack1` | 6-10 | see below | A1 |
| `attack2` / `skill` | 7-11 | | A2 (`skill` = in-place cast, also the victory pose) |
| `attack3` | 8-12 | | A3 |
| `hurt` | 3 | 90-110 ms | lean back, head snaps |
| `death` | 6 | 110-220 ms, last frame 600 ms | kneel, fall, lie still; the game fades it out |
| `rise` | 6 | | only for champions with Undying: the death played backwards, then stand |
| `victory` | 5-8 | | optional: played by the winners at the end of a battle (Gareth's salute, the Rime Scholar's lifted censer); without it the winners play `skill` |

The content tests fail when an animation a skill names is missing, and when the animation's number of hit frames differs from the skill's `hits`.

### 7.2 Attack structure

1. **Anticipation** (1-2 frames, the longest, 130-220 ms): wind up away from the target.
2. **Strike** (1 frame, 50-70 ms): the weapon has already travelled, a **smear** shows the path. This is the **hit frame**.
3. **Follow-through** (1-2 frames, 90-260 ms): overshoot, hold the pose so the impact reads.
4. **Recovery** (2 frames): blend back to idle.

| Event | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| hit frame (`hit: true`) | damage numbers, hurt reactions and impact effects fire here (one per `hits` entry of the skill) |
| `shoot` | a projectile leaves the bow or staff (and Arrow Rain's volley) |
| `jump` | leap take-off; the game moves the champion in an arc that lands exactly on the next hit frame |
| `cast` | spell flash moment; plays the skill's `actorFx` at the head (a howl, a flare) |
| `turn` pose channel | flips the facing for one frame (spins, Mirage Assault) |

### 7.3 Smears

Weapon smears are crescents drawn between the previous and current weapon angle: **thick at the leading edge, tapering to a hairline at the trailing end**, white rim outside, the champion's tint inside (`smearSteel`, `smearGold`, `smearViolet`... in `ACCENT`), taken from the champion's own look or weapon, never from its affinity (2.4): every weapon of the Azure Crown leaves white and the light of its own steel or iron, and so does the Enforcer's sword. They sit behind the body and never exceed ~200 degrees of travel.

### 7.4 Fire on a weapon

A weapon that burns (the Paladin's sword) burns in every frame of every animation, drawn into the sprite:

- Tongues rise from both edges of the blade **up the screen, whichever way the blade points**, and lean away from its travel since the last frame (`info.prev`), longer the faster it moves, so a swing trails fire.
- Each tongue flickers at its own pace, in **whole cycles over a looping animation** (idle, run), so the loop stays seamless; outside the loops they run on at an even pace.
- The fire is light: no outline, no separation lines, and it sits **behind the blade**, so the blade stays the brightest thing. A few embers drift up off it.
- How hot it burns is a pose channel (`fire`: 1 at rest, up to 2 when it roars, 0 when it goes out as he dies).
- The atlas records where the blade burns in each frame (`CharDef.glow`, `glow` in the champion's JSON: its base and tip and the heat), and the game adds soft bands of warm light along it in battle and on every menu that shows the champion, and throws embers off it in battle (`drawBladeGlow` in `src/game/view/unit.ts`, `throwEmbers` in `scene.ts`).

New champions add their effects to the module of their faction.
- An effect shows what the skill does (a cut, a blow, a shield set), never the champion's affinity (2.4).
- Every effect uses `FXR` ramps ending in a near-white core.
- 40-80 ms per frame; impacts are 5-8 frames, set pieces up to 12 (the sarcophagus), looping cues (stun stars, cast circles, projectiles) loop.
- Each effect has an **anchor**: chest-level impacts are centered (`ay 0.5`), ground effects are anchored at the feet (`ay 0.9-1.0`); the battle places any effect with `ay > 0.8` on the target's feet automatically.
- Nothing is cut off by its box. An effect that grows past it (rings at the feet, spikes, bursts, sparks) gets room with `pad: [top, right, bottom, left]`: the drawing keeps its own coordinates, and the built box and anchor include the room, so the effect lands on the same spot. `npm run audit` fails any frame that runs into its box edge, and padding that would move a ground effect's anchor to 0.8 or below.
- Beams from the sky (holy light, sunfire) dissolve toward the top with dithering instead of ending in a straight line.
- Draw layers: `ground` (cast circles, cracks, shockwaves), `world` (depth-sorted with units: ice spikes), `top` (slashes, beams, pillars, numbers).
- Additive blending only for light (holy beams, heal sparkles, hit stars, fire bursts, the revive helix). Solid effects (ice, sand, smoke, the sarcophagus) are drawn normally.
- Hit effects are designed facing right; the battle flips them when the attacker faces left.

A zone is one module exporting a `ZoneArt` (contract in `zones/shared.ts`):

| Part | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Backdrop | 640 x 200 painting: sky with the key light in the upper-left, then distant land; `horizon` marks where land meets sky (heat haze, mist) |

| Budget | backdrop at most 220 colors, tileset at most 160 (`npm run audit`) |

The ravine's far wall faces us with the sun beyond it: strata level along an unnotched lip, darker with depth, fluted, cut by rain gullies, its lip lit; where the gap is wide its floor shows, dark, with a thread of water. The yurts are cylinders seen a little from above under low cones (`yurt()`); the flag poles are loops whose flags each ripple on their own phase down the chain (`flagPole()`); the yaks are drawn from a pose (`yak()`: masses for the barrel, rump and hump, a skirt of hair to the hem, legs from hoof offsets) into grazing and walking frames, and one of them `roam`s. Gusts are loops of sparse lit blades crossing a box and fading (`gust()`), masked to the grass on the backdrop; feather grass sways like Zone 3's reeds, a silky awn trailing from each stem. The zeppelin `drift`s west with a negative speed, its propellers turning; birds are eagles; the ambience is `seeds`. Its ten placeholder stages (4-1 to 4-10) are fought on it.

Zone 5, the Snowy Mesa, named and described by Jakub on 2026-10-19 (DESIGN_DECISIONS.md 5.8: the top of a snow-capped tabletop mountain, many more all round, all connected with many candles, before sunset, a snowstorm approaching from the right corner, a castle on the left mesa with a huge chimney breathing purple dust and particles, a few trees and a big arbiter statue like a Buddha holding a big sword; mid-build the sword moved in front of the hand that holds it, and a dragon's bone added at the front left): the light before sunset, the sun low in the upper left, set in from the corner so the runtime's vignette, darkest there, does not dim its glow. The sea of cloud far below is a height field rendered column by column in true perspective from near to far (`sea()`: a world point at depth Z shows at y = VY + (1 - E) F / Z, so nearer billows hide further ones and each shows a lit top over a shaded flank); it keeps its depth per pixel (`seaZ`), and a mesa's pixel is hidden wherever a billow nearer than the mesa covers it, so the clouds take the mesas' feet; the clouds heap against the mesas and their long shadows lie on them (`seaShadow`). The mesas are drawn far to near from screen boxes (`mesa()`): broad buttresses and fine fluting shaded by the low sun (`sunOn`), soft strata, a snow cap with its cornice's shadow and a few icicles, snow on broken ledges and down the gullies, a top seen from above for those below the eye; the haze takes three hazed copies of the rock and snow ramps. The storm is billows bulging over one another (`stormAt`: the one that bulges nearest at a pixel is seen), a fold of shade where one passes behind another, the front's faces lit rose, solid behind; its veils are ordered-dither streaks, and a far loop drops snow through them. The castle and its huge chimney are painted into the backdrop; the purple dust is a far loop of puffs that rise out of the chimney and stream away left on the wind, each on its own wander, and its motes are a `sparks` emitter along it. The arbiter is drawn with the champion renderer (`arbiter()`: weathered bronze `M_VERD`, his hands, hilt and soles worn to `M_BRONZE`, the blade `M_STEEL`, granite steps, snow primitives on his head, shoulders, lap and guard), the sword in front of the arm that holds it, his robe's folds and curls as textures; the candles on his plinth are a loop in his own box, each flame on a whole number of cycles a loop (`drawCandle`). The pines are pads of needle lumps heaped and capped with snow (`needlePad`), swaying more the higher they grow. The cables from our rim, their posts and swinging lanterns are back loops; the far cables' candles a far loop over the backdrop's lantern pixels; the castle's windows a pulse. The arena shares the backdrop's perspective (`world()`): snow drifts lit on their flanks toward the sun, cooler under the storm, the long shadows of the statue, its blade and the pines (`shadowK`), the terrace's flagstones laid in world space with drifts over them, footprints, rocks with frozen grass; the challengers' swords with their ribbons, the stone lantern and the dragon's skull (the champion renderer again, running off the left edge) are floor props, a bough the near corner's. The ambience is `flurries`; the storm's lightning is the zone's `flash`; choughs are its birds. Its ten placeholder stages (5-1 to 5-10) are fought on it.

Floor tiles repeat across the arena, so a crack or a stain painted into a common variant repeats everywhere: keep damage to one rarely placed variant, and keep organic floors seamless by sharing one base field across variants and fading each variant's own detail in away from the tile border (`grass()` in `zone1.ts`). Large ground features (a worn path, a trampled patch) are `floor` props, not tiles.

## 10a. The main menu's sanctum (`tools/art/sanctum.ts`)

The main menu is not a zone: it has a diorama of its own (Jakub, 2026-10-24: "new and static, designed just for main menu", "very epic", "celestial or planetar"). `npm run art -- menu` writes `public/assets/menu/` (`sky.png`, the whole static sky; `scene.png`, every sprite of the island; `menu.json`, where they go and how they move) and the preview `docs/images/menu_sanctum.png`; `src/game/view/sanctum.ts` draws it.

![The Sanctum](images/menu_sanctum.png)

The sky: near-black space deepening to indigo toward the abyss; a nebula band rising to the right, warped by slow noise so it billows, violet in its core, teal toward its edges and a breath of rose, all dithered into one another and kept low in value, dark lanes winding along it; stars crowding along it; the pole star in the upper left, the key light, a halo and four spikes. The giant planet is a crescent lit from beyond it (the light behind it, so most of it lies in night, the night side faintly lit by its rings), warm and cool bands of uneven width bent by turbulence, a storm, the rings' shadow on the globe, its rings split so the far half passes behind the globe. The Loom's rim is an ellipse whose crown stands above the screen, so it frames the sky from the corners and leaves the title clear: a band of plates of old gold with a channel of runes in arcane light, its outer lip lit by the pole star, a thin rail outward joined by struts, studs of light; it is broken on the right (`BREAK`), its fragments bobbing near the gap, and two veils of its cloth (Zone 1's veil technique and ramp, so the rift and the Loom read as one world) stream from the broken ends on a slow loop. Its warp hangs loose: threads of starlight in a slow S, a bead of light at each end, a few snapped and curling. The golden thread climbs from the Weaver's Spire's spool to the break in a curve that dips under the title.

The island: one sprite in screen space. Its top in grass with glowing flowers, a plaza of flagstones and paths, a gold medallion of the Loom's rose; under its lip a cliff of strata lit from the left, moss hanging, then the great inverted crag of its underside, faceted (a noise's slope stepped into planes), cracked, veins of crystal surfacing here and there, clusters of crystal jutting, roots hanging, the planet's light along the edges that face down and right; waterfalls off both ends that arc over the lip and fray into spray. Each place is its own sprite, so its pixels are what the pointer hits and an outline can be made from its silhouette (`outlineOf`: a bright ring just outside it, a dimmer one beyond): the Starbridge Gate (fluted pillars, voussoirs, a jewelled keystone, a gold star; its portal a loop of violet depths with stars streaming in and a road of light running into them), the Reliquary (`ashlar()` courses, belfries and slate spires, a gable with crockets, the rose window as the logo's rose in stained glass, a gallery of figures, a portal of archivolts round a door ajar on candlelight), the Weaver's Spire (an octagon of three lit faces, a gallery of gold columns, its spool a loop: a crystal spool, its thread spiralling, an armillary of two rings turning round it), the Observatory (a lit cylinder, a dome of green copper ribbed in gold, a brass telescope out of its slit, a library wing), and the astrolabe on a drifting rock. Windows, the rose window and the lanterns breathe (`pulse` overlays); pools of light breathe over the portal, the rose window, the spool and the lanterns (`lights`). Everything that loops runs whole cycles per loop; everything gigantic (the rim, its veils) moves slowly.
