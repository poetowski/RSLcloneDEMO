# The Loom: Reliquary of Legends — guide for coding agents

A turn-based hero battler (RAID / SWGOH style) in TypeScript + Canvas. All art is generated by code in `tools/art` into `public/assets` (versioned). Quality bar: every change is reviewed visually, in screenshots, before it is done.

## Content approval

The game is **The Loom: Reliquary of Legends** (code name TLROL), and its content is Jakub's. Every decision he has made is in [docs/DESIGN_DECISIONS.md](docs/DESIGN_DECISIONS.md), the one source for content work: the premise, enums and factions (1-3, 6), the starters (4, 8), the Skirmisher (12), the Lineholder (13), the Quartermaster (14), the Enforcer (15), the Bloodhound (16), the Dominion Medic (17), the Follower (18), the Walking Bush (19), the Wild Speaker (20), the Jungle Herder (21), Emissary Handlings (22), Red Cloak (23), Big Ears (24), the Associate (25), the Black Rhino (26), the Grove Keeper (27), the Skeleton (28), the Rot Hulk (29), the Bellringer (30), the Necromancer (31), the Automated Soul (32), Irving Wellbington (33), the Paladin (34), the Challenges and the tutorial (35), summoning (36), Chief Engineer CJ (37), the campaign, Zone 1, Zone 2's background and placeholder stages, Zone 3 (the Moonlit Basin: his name and description), Zone 4 (Earth Scars: his name and description), Zone 5 (the Snowy Mesa: his name and description) and the map (5), the Weaver Matrix, Weaving and the spool drops, and the Spindle of Fate as a placeholder (9), the combat rules and stats (10), the name, direction and lexicon (11); section 7 lists what is not decided. Jakub, the game's designer, approves or rejects every piece of real content before it is built: names, stats, numbers, mechanics, texts and art. On 2026-10-11 he said of the game as it looked then (Zone 1, the champions, the matrix): "i like how it looks now in game", and of the redrawn world map: "i love it"; new art is still proposed with previews first.

## Rules

- Affinity is abstract (Jakub, 2026-10-11): it never dictates a champion's colours, materials, elements or effects; the look and the effects come from who the champion is and what the skill does (DESIGN_DECISIONS.md 2.1, ART_GUIDE 2.4).
- A passive that raises a stat is extra to the stat budget and applies in combat only: the collection and the champion page show the stats without it (Jakub, 2026-10-12; DESIGN_DECISIONS.md 10). So is Big Ears' Hidden in the Undergrowth, which grows as his allies fall (`kind: 'fallen'`), and the Skeleton's Bare Bones, +20 RES.
- A passive that acts in the flow of a fight (`tend`, `ward`, `retaliate`, `remedy`) names its effects in its data (`fx` on whom it reaches, `holderFx` on its holder) and shows its name over its holder without a banner.
- One key light, top-left, for everything; sprites are rendered for both facings, never mirrored.
- No color literals in champion modules: colors come from `MAT`, `ACCENT`, `INK` in `palette.ts`.
- Numbers stay inside `src/game/data/norms.ts`; tune stage `power` with `npm run balance`.
- A rule change updates its test, `docs/MECHANICS_GUIDE.md` and the Academy text in `src/game/data/codex.ts` together.
- A change to mechanics, content or the domain model fills in the MDA impact card (`docs/MDA.md` section 7) and keeps `docs/DDD.md` true (glossary, invariants, drift register).
- Skill `hits` match the animation's hit frames; effect names must exist (content tests check both).
- The tutorial finds its spots by the screens' region ids (`m_<place>`, `loc_<area>`, `st_<stage>`, `p_go`, `fight`, `skill<i>`, `r_next`, `r_retry`, `go`, `back`, `tab_journey`, `q_act`, `q_<quest>`, and for the lesson on the first summon `merge`, `summon` and the Wishing Star's `wishSpot` and `dustSpot`): renaming one, or moving a screen the tutorial walks, means updating `src/game/tutorial.ts` and running `tools/tutorialrun.ts`.
- Regenerate and commit `public/assets` with the code that needs it.
- Pixel art only: integer coordinates, no smooth gradients or anti-aliased shapes (use `ui.bands`, `ui.glow`, dithering).
- Text uses the four faces of the one pixel font: `regular`, `bold`, `display` for titles, names in headers and big numbers, and `micro` digits for counters on status icons; never scale `regular` or `bold` up. A new character in any text needs a glyph (content tests check).
